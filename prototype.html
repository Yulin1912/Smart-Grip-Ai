Prototype lab / Proof of concept

Four prototype levels.
One ethical rule.

The project is still a designed system, not a validated commercial welfare product. Size and mounting are proven separately from “can this sensing idea collect useful events?” Never create extra disturbance to test the device.

Level A

Portfolio

3D render, exploded view, app mockup, architecture and this site. Communicate design intent without claiming a manufactured unit.

You are looking at Level A.

Level B

Bench

ESP32 development board + sensor modules + LED. The Tracer-like RestGuard tracker is this chapter: prove ranging, RMS, reed and logic.

Success: repeated scripted events produce repeatable, distinguishable traces.

Level C

Appearance

3D-printed cream shell with a non-functional screen insert. Evaluate size, mounting, visitor glance and staff reach.

Success: no exposed edge inside the enclosure; strap survives a pull test.

Level D

Pilot unit

Custom PCB and a supervised site installation — only after empty-enclosure or simulated storefront work, with professional approval.

Success: reliability and workflow evidence, not a marketing claim.

Test plan

A strong portfolio shows how the idea could fail.

01 Approach

≥90% detection

10 participants × 10 approaches at varied angles. Pass-by false counts under 10%.

02 Door

≥99 / 100 events

Open/close fixture. Magnet alignment is part of the test, not an afterthought.

03 Latency

≤2 seconds

Timestamp the sensor event and the display change. Visitors should not wait on the ring.

04 Privacy

No raw audio

Firmware and payload audit: no waveform, image or identifier present.

05 Usability

≥80% task success

Five staff complete three tasks without instruction: rest, reset, read the breakdown.

06 Mounting

No loose part

Inspect edges. Controlled pull on the strap. Nothing accessible inside the enclosure.

Pilot rule Never create extra disturbance to test the device; begin with simulations and staff-approved observation.

Limits

Write the failures in before the gallery does.

  • False green must never be read as permission to touch.
  • False rest can restrict without cause — hysteresis and a manual reset are required.
  • Optical reflection on glass is a first-class calibration risk.
  • The score has not been correlated with species-specific welfare outcomes.
  • Fire rating, ingress, EMC, cleaning chemicals and care policy are future engineering.

Portfolio statement

RestGuard is a designed system — not yet a validated welfare product.

It combines product design, embedded sensing, interaction design, privacy and test engineering to turn hidden enclosure–visitor events into measurable, humane guidance. Current position: concept and portfolio prototype. Next: calibration, false-count measurement, and whether the display actually changes behaviour.
